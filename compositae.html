Find the fruit and count its ribs

Count around the whole fruit

Use a hand lens and light from the side. Pick one raised ridge, then slowly roll the ripe fruit until you reach that ridge again. Each lengthwise ridge counts once. Skip the grooves between ridges and the bristles on top.

    A1 Flora p. 10

    A6 Flora p. 10

    A. Bracts around the head are dry and papery, at least along their edges. → A7

    B. This combination of branch shape, small stalk bracts and narrow head bracts is absent. → A9

    A9 Flora p. 10,12

    A. Leaves very narrow and toothless; ripe fruits curve inward, stick out of the head and spread like a star; hooked bristles on their backs and tips. → Koelpinia

    B. Leaves widest above the middle, usually toothed; ripe fruits stay inside the bracts around the head. → A10

    A10 Flora p. 12

    A. Ripe head roughly round; outer fruits have a little crown and are wrapped in bracts; inner fruits have pointed scales about half the fruit length; flowers yellow. → Hedypnois

    B. Ripe head oblong; outer fruits not wrapped in bracts; all fruits have short scales about one-fifth the fruit length; flowers blue, rarely white. → Cichorium

    A11 Flora p. 12

    A. Hairs of the inner fruits' tuft have side branches like a feather (their bases may be broad and scale-like). → A12

    A12 Flora p. 12

    B. Fruit tops are flat, gradually narrowed, or only very shortly beaked. → A18

    A13 Flora p. 12

    A. Head bracts in one row, or in 2–3 rows but similar in shape and nearly equal in length. → A14

    A14 Flora p. 12

    A. All fruits have hairy tufts; the flower platform is bare or has a few short hairs. → A15

    A15 Flora p. 12

    A. Leaf side veins form a branching network; leaves rarely toothless; head bracts joined below; fruits somewhat flattened, with a swelling at the beak's base. → Urospermum

    A16 Flora p. 12

    A. Small, narrow, pointed, thin scales stand between flowers on the platform. → Hypochoeris

    B. The flower platform has no scales between the flowers. → A17

    A17 Flora p. 12

    A. Outer bracts egg-shaped with a heart-shaped base, about as long as the narrow inner bracts. → Helminthotheca

    B. Outer and inner bracts similar in shape and overlap like roof tiles. → Leontodon

    A18 Flora p. 12

    A. At least some plant hairs are branched; outer fruits sometimes have a crown or fringe instead of a feathery tuft. → A19

    B. Any plant hairs are unbranched, usually woolly; all fruits have a feathery tuft. → Scorzonera

    A19 Flora p. 12

    A. Outer fruits fairly straight, with lines or ribs; tuft stays attached; plant hairs usually include stalked, forked hairs. → Leontodon

    B. Outer fruits often curved, with 5 faint ribs; tuft usually falls; plant hairs include tiny anchor-shaped hairs with hooks. → Picris

    A20 Flora p. 12

    A. Outer fruits curve inward and are longer than the head's bract cup; top fringe of hairs only about 0.2 mm long. → Rhagadiolus

    B. Fruits shorter than the head's bract cup and hidden inside it; tuft hairs longer. → A21

    A21 Flora p. 12,13

    A. Fruit top has a beak, or narrows into a short beak. → A22

    B. Fruit top has no beak: it is rounded or flat. → A32

    A22 Flora p. 12,13

    A. Usually one head (rarely a few) on a leafless stalk; leaves in a ground-level rosette. → A23

    B. Several or many heads on leafy stems. → A24

    A23 Flora p. 12,13

    A. Upper part of fruit has small bumps or prickles. → Taraxacum

    B. Fruit smooth or covered throughout with fine pointed projections. → Crepis

    A24 Flora p. 13

    A. Fruit roughly round or angled in cross-section; beak tip a little thicker, but not widened into a flat disc. → A25

    B. Fruit flattened; beak tip widened into a flat disc carrying the tuft. → A26

    A25 Flora p. 13

    A. Beak has a joint and falls off; heads almost stalkless on wand-like branches, or branches spread widely and the head stalks stay as spines. → Chondrilla

    B. Beak has no joint and stays attached; head stalks do not become spines and heads are never all almost stalkless. → Crepis

    A26 Flora p. 13

    A. Middle and upper leaves have narrow ear-like bases running 0.3–3.5 cm down the stem; heads have 4–8 yellow flowers. → Scariola

    A27 Flora p. 13

    A. Tuft in two sets: a tiny outer ring of smooth hairs 0.1–0.3 mm long, plus many long, fine, rough or barbed inner hairs. Use a lens. → A28

    B. Tuft in one set of many long, fine, roughly equal hairs, rough or finely barbed. → A31

    A28 Flora p. 13

    A. 7–9 head bracts in two rows; outer ones very short, inner ones much longer, making a narrow cylinder; flowers yellow; short creeping underground stem. → Mycelis

    A29 Flora p. 13

    A. Fruit beak stout, 0.2–0.5 mm long, the same colour as the fruit or paler; a taproot or creeping underground stem. → Cicerbita

    B. Fruit beak slender, 1.7–14.5 mm long and paler than the fruit; a swollen rootstock. → A30

    A30 Flora p. 13

    A. Bract cup 8.5–17 mm long; fruits up to 1 mm wide, only slightly flattened; beak 1.7–3.5 mm; inner tuft 4–6 mm. → Cephalorrhynchus

    B. Bract cup 18–36 mm long; fruits 2–3 mm wide and strongly flattened; beak 8.5–14.5 mm; inner tuft 10.5–12 mm. → Steptorhamphus

    A31 Flora p. 13

    A. Beak stout, 2–4 mm, the same colour as the fruit; fruit slightly flattened and narrows gradually into the beak. → Mulgedium

    B. Beak slender, 2.5–13 mm, usually paler; fruit strongly flattened and narrows rather suddenly into the beak. → Lactuca

    A32 Flora p. 13,14

    A. At least some hairs are star-shaped; fruits clearly have 10 ribs; tuft dirty-white or brownish. → A33

    B. Hairs, if present, are ordinary or gland-tipped, never star-shaped; fruit rib numbers vary (sometimes 10); tuft usually white, sometimes yellow or brownish. → A35

    A33 Flora p. 14

    A. Fruit ribs reach the top, making a slightly scalloped edge; tuft in one row; runners may be present. → A34

    B. Fruit ribs meet a faint ring at the top with a smooth edge; tuft in two rows; perennial without runners. → Hieracium

    A34 Flora p. 14

    A. Leaves mostly in a basal rosette and green at least above; perennial, usually with runners; rough tuft hairs remain but break easily. → Pilosella

    B. Leaves white or golden from many star-shaped hairs, not mostly basal; annual without runners; tuft hairs barbed near their bases and fall off. → Andryala

    A35 Flora p. 14

    A. Slender perennial with runners; most leaves at the base; usually one head on each leafless stalk. → Aetheorhiza

    A36 Flora p. 14

    A. Fruits oblong or column-shaped, roughly round in cross-section or only somewhat flattened. → A37

    A37 Flora p. 14

    A38 Flora p. 14

    A. Tuft hairs rather stiff, like bristles; leaf veins roughly parallel. → Scorzonera

    B. Tuft hairs soft; side veins branch from the leaf's middle vein. → A39

    A39 Flora p. 14

    A. Fruits have 3–4 ribs; flowers usually purple. Exception: P. abietina is yellow, with broad leaves bearing sideways basal lobes; bracts overlap in gradually increasing lengths. → Prenanthes

    A40 Flora p. 14

    A. Fruits smooth or finely pointed all over; inner bracts never have little horns below their tips. → Crepis

    Group B — prickly leaves or head bracts
    B1 Flora p. 14,15

    B24 Flora p. 16

    A. Flower platform deeply pitted with little teeth around the pits. → Onopordum

    B. Platform not deeply pitted; it has scales, bristles or long hairs. → B25

    B25 Flora p. 16,17

    A. Leaves not spiny. → B26

    B. Leaves spiny. → B29

    B26 Flora p. 16

    B. Long hairs stand on the flower platform. → B28

    B27 Flora p. 16

    B. Tuft has many finely barbed hairs; inner bracts hardly papery, greenish or straw-coloured; perennial. → Jurinea

    B28 Flora p. 16

    A. Inner fruits have one longer hair in the middle of the tuft; inner bracts have a purplish spine. → Oligochaeta

    B29 Flora p. 17

    A. Scales stand between flowers on the platform. → B30

    B30 Flora p. 17

    B. Fruit top has a little crown; head bracts have thin papery edges and no spines. → Anthemis

    B31 Flora p. 17

    B32 Flora p. 17

    B. Tuft hairs joined at their bases into a ring and fall with it. → B34

    B. Stems with wings; head bract edges not spiny. → B35

    B35 Flora p. 17

    C3 Flora p. 17

    C4 Flora p. 17

    B. Scales between flowers broader and pointed; fruit top has 2 pointed scales that fall off; cultivated. → Helianthus

    C5 Flora p. 17,18

    C6 Flora p. 17

    C7 Flora p. 18

    C8 Flora p. 18

    B. No fruit tuft, or almost none; no hairy tuft. → C17

    C9 Flora p. 18

    C10 Flora p. 18

    B. One, several or many heads on leafy stems. → C11

    C11 Flora p. 18

    B. Head bracts oblong, pointed and upright, touching along their sides; usually a little outer ring of separate bracts near the base. → Senecio

    C12 Flora p. 18

    A. Tiny outer scales form a ring at the bases of the tuft hairs. → Pulicaria

    B. Fruit tuft consists only of hairs. → C13

    C13 Flora p. 18

    A. Outer straps tiny and inconspicuous, only about as long as the tuft hairs. → C14

    B. Outer straps obvious and longer than the tuft hairs. → C15

    C14 Flora p. 18

    C15 Flora p. 18

    B. No fine network of dark veins under the leaves; style branch tips rounded or flat. → C16

    Group D — white, pink, purple or other outer straps
    D1 Flora p. 19

    A. Scales stand between the flowers, at least on the inner part of the platform (faintly developed in Uechtritzia); scales broad or narrow and bristle-like. → D2

    B. Flower platform bare. → D9

    D2 Flora p. 19

    A. All leaves basal, egg-shaped with heart-shaped bases; leafless flower stalks; flowers deep pink-red; inner flowers two-lipped and at least 15 mm long. → Uechtritzia

    B. Stems leafy; leaves not egg-shaped with heart-shaped bases; outer flowers white, pink or purple; central flowers usually yellow tubes, or slightly two-lipped but no more than 6 mm long. → D3

    D3 Flora p. 19

    A. All leaves opposite, undivided and nearly toothless; scales narrow, bristle-like and falling off; annual of marshy ground. → Eclipta

    B. Leaves alternate, usually deeply divided 1–3 times, rarely undivided; scales broad; annual or perennial. → D4

    D4 Flora p. 19

    A. Fruits flattened from front to back, with two distinct edge ribs or wings. → D5

    B. Fruits round or angled in cross-section, or somewhat flattened, without distinct edge ribs or wings. → D8

    D5 Flora p. 19

    A. At least the outer central fruits have thin papery or see-through wings. → D6

    B. Outer central fruits, or all fruits, have distinct edge ribs but no wings. → D7

    D6 Flora p. 19

    A. Central flower tube base split into two lobes clasping the fruit top; leaves very narrow in outline and deeply divided 2–3 times, with crowded divisions making a worm-like shape. → Leucocyclus

    B. Central flower tube base not split into lobes, attached to front of the fruit; leaves oblong to egg-shaped in outline, deeply divided twice, divisions not crowded. → Anacyclus

    D7 Flora p. 19

    A. Central flower tube base forms a pouch around fruit top; fruit rounded and without a crown; scales' middle ribs stop before their tips. → Achillea

    B. Central flower tube base not pouched; fruit top has a short crown; scales' middle ribs extend into pointed tips. → Anthemis

    Botanical word guide
    Flower head (capitulum)
    The whole daisy-like or thistle-like head. It is made of many tiny individual flowers, called florets.
    Outer strap (ligule or ray)
    A flat, petal-like part of a tiny flower. A dandelion has straps throughout its head; a daisy has them only around the outside.
    Head bracts (phyllaries)
    The small leaf-like or papery pieces around the outside and base of a flower head.
    Bract cup (involucre)
    All the head bracts taken together. Measure across or along this cup only, not including the outer flower straps.
    Flower platform (receptacle)
    The solid base inside the head where the little flowers attach. Open a head carefully lengthwise or remove some flowers to see it.
    Scales between flowers (paleae)
    Small bracts attached to the flower platform between individual flowers. They stay on the platform when a fruit is removed.
    Fruit (achene)
    The little dry, one-seeded fruit often called a seed. Use ripe fruits where the key asks about shape, ribs, beaks or hairs.
    Fruit tuft (pappus)
    Hairs, bristles, scales or a tiny crown attached to the TOP OF EACH FRUIT. These are different from hairs or scales attached to the flower platform.
    Feathery (plumose)
    A hair or bristle with many fine side branches, like a feather. Check with a lens.
    Beak
    A narrow extension at the top of the fruit, below its tuft.
    Attachment scar (hilum)
    The little mark near the base or side of a fruit where it was attached to the flower platform.
    Style
    The slender part rising from the top of the fruit inside a flower; its tip often forks into two branches.
    Pollen sacs (anthers)
    The pollen-bearing parts of a flower. Their stalks are called filaments. Tiny tails at the bases of pollen sacs need close magnification.
    Bisexual flower
    One tiny flower with both male and female parts. Female flowers lack working male parts; sterile flowers do not produce fruits.
    Opposite / alternate leaves
    Opposite leaves come in pairs at the same point on a stem. Alternate leaves are attached singly, at different heights.
    Rosette / basal leaves
    Leaves crowded near ground level. Stem leaves grow higher up the stem.
    Divided once, twice or three times
    A divided leaf has deep cuts toward its middle vein. If those divisions are cut again, it is divided twice; a further round makes three times.
    Wing
    A flat strip along a stem or fruit, not an insect wing.
    Annual / biennial / perennial
    An annual completes its life in one growing season; a biennial usually takes two; a perennial lives for several years.
    Runner / underground stem
    A runner creeps along the ground and roots at joints. An underground stem (rhizome) grows below the soil.
    Gland / glandular
    A small structure producing sticky liquid or other substances. Some glands are on the tips of hairs.
    Papery / felted / cobwebby
    Papery parts are thin and dry. Felted hairs make a dense soft covering. Cobwebby hairs form a loose, fine web.
    Cross-section
    The shape seen looking at a cut end. A fruit can be round, angular or flattened across its width.
    mm / cm
    10 millimetres (mm) = 1 centimetre (cm). Use a small ruler and a hand lens.
