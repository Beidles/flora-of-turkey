Clinopodium of Türkiye

Interactive plant identification key · Two species · Includes C. vulgare subspecies · Simple English
← All plant keys
How to use

This key is for plants already identified as Clinopodium, following the older Flora of Turkey treatment. Use a flowering plant, a millimetre ruler and a hand lens. Measure several fully opened flowers and green flower cups. At each step, tap the description that fits.

Step 1 identifies the species. If you reach Clinopodium vulgare, Step 2 helps identify its subspecies. If measurements sit on the shared boundaries, compare all the characters before deciding.

Possible identification

Check all the features before deciding. The two C. vulgare subspecies can be intermediate. At shared measurement boundaries, or if the tooth sizes disagree, keep the name Clinopodium vulgare without choosing a subspecies.

Words and measurements explained

Corolla: the coloured part of a flower. Measure its whole length from the base of the tube to the lip tips. Calyx: the green cup around the flower base; measure from its base to the longest tooth tip. The calyx tube is the part below the teeth. Measure a tooth from the notch at its base to its tip.

Bracteoles: tiny, narrow, leaf-like parts beside the flowers. Pedicel: the short stalk of one flower. Flower cluster: a group around the stem at a leaf joint. Subspecies: a named group within a species.

Check the genus first: Davis describes perennial herbs with broad, toothed, short-stalked leaves; a curved, hairy green flower cup; long hairs along the lower cup teeth; and usually pink or purple flowers, rarely white. These are supporting features, not a complete genus-identification key.

This follows the historical Clinopodium treatment in the book. It does not include species placed in other genera there, such as Calamintha and Acinos. Use those separate keys for that material.

Source: P. H. Davis (ed.), Flora of Turkey and the East Aegean Islands, Volume 7, printed pages 329–330; Clinopodium account by E. Leblebici.
