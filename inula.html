    Inula — species key
    I1 Flora p. 54–55

    1. No female flowers around the outside of the head, or the outer flowers are tubes or tiny straps no longer than the central tube flowers. → I2

    2. Outer female flowers have flat, petal-like straps longer than the central tube flowers. These straps may be short and hard to notice, about 3 mm long. → I11

    I2 Flora p. 54

    1. All flowers in the head have both male and female parts; no separate female flowers at all. → I3

    2. A few separate female flowers form a ring around the head; they are tube-shaped. → I7

    I3 Flora p. 54

    1. Tall, coarse herb up to 2 m; basal leaves very large, 30–50 cm long and 15–25 cm wide; many heads in fairly flat-topped clusters. → Inula peacockiana

    2. Smaller herb, usually 15–50 cm, sometimes to 100 cm; basal leaves seldom over 15 cm long and 5 cm wide; usually several heads, no more than 15, along an elongated cluster. → I4

    I4 Flora p. 54

    1. Dense white woolly or felt-like hairs; stems 15–40 cm tall. → I5

    2. Plant has gland-tipped hairs or ordinary soft hairs, without white felt; stems usually 30–60 cm, sometimes to 100 cm. → I6

    I5 Flora p. 54

    1. Bract cup around the head about 1 cm wide; southwestern Anatolia. → Inula heterolepis

    2. Bract cup about 1.5 cm wide; eastern Anatolia. → Inula fragilis

    I6 Flora p. 54

    1. Plant sticky; several small leaf-like pieces surround the head and are a little longer than the bract cup. → Inula inuloides

    2. Plant finely soft-haired; no extra small leaves surrounding the head. → Inula discoidea

    I7 Flora p. 54

    1. At least the lower leaves densely covered with greyish-white felt-like hairs. → I8

    2. Leaves have ordinary or gland-tipped hairs, without greyish-white felt. → I9

    I8 Flora p. 54

    1. Leaves widest above the middle and tapering toward the base; upper leaves greenish; outer head bracts pointed and greyish. → Inula anatolica

    2. Leaves egg-shaped to oval; upper leaves whitish and densely felted; outer head bracts oblong, blunt and white-felted. → Inula heterolepis

    I9 Flora p. 54

    1. Plant sticky from glands; bract cup 1.5–2 cm wide. → Inula sarana

    2. Plant not sticky, with few or no glands; bract cup 1 cm wide or less. → I10

    I10 Flora p. 54

    1. Head bracts narrow and pointed, their tips barely green and leaf-like; leaf bases often continue well down the stem as strips. → Inula thapsoides

    2. Head bracts egg-shaped with green, spreading, leaf-like tips; leaf bases do not continue down the stem. → Inula vulgaris

    I11 Flora p. 55

    1. Outer head bracts broad, 5–14 mm wide; fruits have 4–5 angles; tuft hairs joined together for the lowest 0.2–0.3 mm. → I12

    2. Outer head bracts under 4 mm wide; fruits roughly round in cross-section and finely ribbed; tuft hairs separate, or joined only very briefly at the base. → I13

    I12 Flora p. 55

    1. Tall herb, 1–2 m, with 5–20 heads in flat-topped or elongated clusters; basal leaves 40–50 cm long and 12–20 cm wide. → H1

    2. Medium herb, 30–60 cm, with 1–2 heads; basal leaves 15–25 cm long and 3–5.5 cm wide. → Inula macrocephala

    I13 Flora p. 55

    1. Bract cup small, 1 cm wide or less; several to many heads; outer straps 5–6 mm long or shorter, inconspicuous and not much longer than the bract cup. → I14

    2. Bract cup larger, 1.5 cm wide or more; heads single or several; outer straps usually over 1 cm long, obvious and clearly longer than the bract cup. → I18

    I14 Flora p. 55

    1. Plant densely covered with greyish-white felt-like hairs. → Inula aschersoniana

    2. Plant basically green: glandular, hairy or hairless. → I15

    I15 Flora p. 55

    1. Leaves egg-shaped, narrow and pointed, or widest above the middle; usually 1–2 cm wide. → I16

    2. Leaves very narrow and pointed or spoon-shaped; usually under 5 mm wide. → I17

    I16 Flora p. 55

    1. Sticky plant, woody near the base; leaf bases narrowed with small ear-like lobes; heads in loose elongated or branching clusters. → Inula viscosa

    2. Herb with long soft hairs; leaf bases rounded or heart-shaped; heads in fairly flat-topped clusters. → Inula germanica

    I17 Flora p. 55

    1. Annual with glands; leaves flat and not fleshy. → Inula graveolens

    2. Small woody plant without hairs; leaves clearly fleshy. → Inula crithmoides

    I18 Flora p. 55

    1. Leaves narrow and grass-like, with veins running almost parallel. → Inula ensifolia

    2. Leaves variously shaped; side veins branch out from the middle vein. → I19

    I19 Flora p. 55

    1. Plant stemless or with a short stem up to 18 cm; usually one head, rarely a few. → Inula acaulis

    2. Stems generally over 20 cm; usually more than one head, except in I. orientalis, I. salicina and I. montbretiana. → I20

    I20 Flora p. 55

    1. Head bracts densely covered with spreading dark-brown hairs; heads always single. → Inula orientalis

    2. Head bracts lack that dense covering of spreading dark-brown hairs; heads generally several or many. → I21

    I21 Flora p. 55

    1. Leaves hairless or with only a few scattered hairs. → I22

    2. Leaves clearly soft-haired or long-haired, sometimes densely hairy and greyish. → I24

    I22 Flora p. 55

    1. Often one head, sometimes a few; bract cup without glands; head bract edges have fine brownish hairs. → Inula salicina

    2. Usually several heads; bract cup with scattered or many glands. → I23

    I23 Flora p. 55

    1. Outer head bracts leaf-like, spreading and with a few glands; leaves bright green, their edges very finely rough like a tiny saw. → Inula viscidula

    2. Outer head bracts hardly leaf-like, pressed close or spreading only at their tips; leaves often yellowish-green with a few hairs along the edges. → Inula aucherana

    I24 Flora p. 55–56

    1. Head bracts loose or spreading away from the head. → I25

    2. Head bracts mostly pressed close against the head. → I26

    I25 Flora p. 55–56

    1. Bract cup 2–3 cm wide; bracts covered with long whitish or yellowish hairs spreading outward. → Inula mariae

    2. Bract cup 1.2–1.5 cm wide; inner bracts finely soft-haired and glandular. → Inula britannica

    I26 Flora p. 56

    1. Stem leaves taper toward their bases; usually one head, sometimes 2–3; head bracts egg-shaped. → Inula montbretiana

    2. Stem leaves broad, with heart-shaped bases or ear-like lobes; usually several heads, rarely one; head bracts very narrow, widest above the middle. → Inula oculus-christi

    Inula helenium — subspecies key
    H1 Flora p. 56

    1. Outer head bracts clearly leaf-like and longer than the inner ones; bract cup 3.5–5 cm wide; outer flower straps 25–30 mm long. → Inula helenium subsp. orgyalis

    2. Outer head bracts hardly leaf-like and shorter than the inner ones; bract cup 2–3 cm wide; outer flower straps under 20 mm long. → H2

    H2 Flora p. 56

    1. Only about 15 outer straps, each 3–6 mm long and barely longer than the bract cup. → Inula helenium subsp. vanensis

    2. Many outer straps, each 10–15 mm long and clearly longer than the bract cup. → H3

    H3 Flora p. 56

    1. Heads on short side branches, making an elongated, spike-like or raceme-like cluster. → Inula helenium subsp. turcoracemosa

    2. Heads on long side branches, making a fairly flat-topped cluster. → Inula helenium subsp. pseudohelenium

    Before you start

    For plants already identified as Inula using the historical Flora of Turkey treatment. This key includes all 26 species from your pages and the four subspecies of Inula helenium.

    Source: A. J. Grierson, Inula, in P. H. Davis (editor), Flora of Turkey and the East Aegean Islands, Volume 5 (1975), printed pages 54–56.

    Use a flowering plant, a ruler and a hand lens. Ripe fruits help with question I11. Check several leaves and heads where possible.

    Measure the right part: the bract cup is the wrapper of small leaf-like pieces beneath a head. Its width excludes the yellow outer straps. Measure an outer strap from its base to its tip.

    Names follow the book. Some plants have since been placed in other genera. Keep the measurements and other features together when choosing; confirm your result against its species description. This page contains keys, not full descriptions of all species.

    Botanical word guide
    Head (capitulum)
    A whole daisy-like head made of many tiny individual flowers.
    Central flowers (disc flowers)
    The small tube-shaped flowers in the middle of the head.
    Outer straps (ligules)
    The flat yellow, petal-like parts of the outer flowers. Sometimes these are tiny or absent.
    Female flowers
    Tiny flowers that have female parts but no working pollen sacs. With a lens, look for a slender style, often forked at the tip, and no pollen-sac tube.
    Bisexual flowers
    Tiny flowers with both male and female parts: a pollen-sac tube and a style.
    Head bracts (phyllaries)
    The small overlapping leaf-like pieces on the outside and underside of the head. Outer bracts are farthest from the centre; inner ones are nearest the flowers.
    Bract cup (involucre)
    All the head bracts together. Measure its width across the wrapper, leaving out the yellow flower straps.
    Basal / stem leaves
    Basal leaves grow near ground level. Stem leaves grow higher up the stem.
    Leaf base running down the stem (decurrent)
    The lower edges of a leaf continue down the stem as narrow strips.
    Ear-like lobes (auricles)
    Small projecting lobes on either side of the leaf base, beside the stem.
    Glands / glandular
    Tiny structures that release liquid; some are at the tips of hairs. Many glands can make a plant sticky.
    Felt-like / woolly hairs
    A thick, matted covering. A few scattered hairs do not count as a dense felt.
    Fruit / tuft
    A fruit is the little dry, one-seeded body often called a seed. Its tuft (pappus) is the set of hairs on top.
    Annual / perennial / subshrub
    An annual lives for one growing season. A perennial lives for several years. A subshrub is a small plant with a woody base.
    Subspecies
    A named form within a species. The four helenium results all belong to Inula helenium.
    mm / cm
    10 millimetres (mm) = 1 centimetre (cm). A length followed by a width describes one leaf, not two separate leaves.
