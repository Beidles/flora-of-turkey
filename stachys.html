How to use

This is the full main species key for Stachys in Flora of Turkey, including the five groups A–E and the former Betonica species. First confirm that your plant belongs to Stachys. Use a flowering plant, a millimetre ruler and a hand lens. Some choices need a developing fruit, a look inside a flower tube, or a microscope for branched hairs.

Tap the description that fits the whole set of features. If something cannot be seen, use Go back; do not guess. “About” and overlapping ranges in the original key are retained. A numbered group is a route through the key, not a final identification. This page ends at the printed main-key names; further subspecies and variety keys within species accounts are not included.

Words explained

Calyx: green cup at the flower base. Corolla: coloured flower, including its tube and lips. Cup tube: the green part below the teeth; compare teeth to this, not to the whole cup. Bracteoles: tiny leaf-like parts beside individual flowers. Flowering leaves: leaves beside flower clusters. Cluster / flower ring: flowers together at a stem joint (verticillaster).

Rosette: leaves clustered near ground level. Non-flowering shoot: leafy shoot without flowers. Annual: completes its life in one season. Perennial: lives for several years. Rhizome: underground spreading stem. Do not dig up a plant just to answer a question; use other visible evidence or leave it uncertain.

Gland-tipped hairs: hairs with little rounded glands at the ends. Surface glands: glands sitting directly on the plant. Felted: dense matted hairs. Woolly: long tangled hairs. Cobweb-like: thin tangled threads. Star-shaped / tree-like: hairs branched from a centre or along a main hair; a microscope may be needed. Backward-pointing: toward the stem base; forward-pointing: toward its tip.

Lower / middle stem leaves: compare leaves from that part of a flowering stem, not just ground leaves. Heart-shaped base: two rounded lobes beside the leaf stalk. Wedge-shaped: narrows toward the stalk. Oblong: longer than wide, with roughly parallel sides. Lance-shaped: narrow and tapering to a point. Awl-shaped: narrow, tapering like a long sharp spike. Widest above the middle: broader toward the tip than the base. Stamens: pollen-bearing parts inside the flower.
