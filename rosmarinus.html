Before you start

Flora of Turkey includes one species in this genus: Rosmarinus officinalis. There is no choice between Turkish Rosmarinus species in the book. These questions help check a plant you already suspect is rosemary; they do not separate every similar plant.

Use a flowering twig, a ruler marked in millimetres and, if possible, a hand lens.

Words explained

Evergreen: keeps its leaves through winter. Rolled-under edges: the sides of a leaf curl toward its underside. Calyx: the little cup surrounding the base of a flower. Stamens: the flower parts that carry pollen. Felt-like hairs: a close covering of soft, tangled hairs.

Where and when: the book records flowering in February–May and habitats including hills, roadsides and vineyards, at 30–250 metres. Rosemary is also cultivated. These dates and places support an identification; they are not compulsory tests.

Source: P. H. Davis (ed.), Flora of Turkey and the East Aegean Islands, volume 7, Rosmarinus account by R. R. Mill, printed pages 75–76. Names and measurements follow that treatment. This is a newly written feature check, not an original branching species key from the book. Works offline when saved.
