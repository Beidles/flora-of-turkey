🌿 Lavandula — Lavender key

Flora of Turkey, Volume 7 · easy, step-by-step species and subspecies identification

Look at the flower spike near the top of the plant. Choose A or B. You will reach one species, and for Lavandula stoechas you can continue to its subspecies.

This uses the 1982 book's treatment: two species, including cultivated L. angustifolia. It is not a key to all lavender species grown in gardens today.

Simple explanations

Flower spike: a long cluster of flowers near a stem tip. Bract: a small leaf-like piece among the flowers. Coma: the showy tuft of larger bracts above a lavender flower spike, often purple. Peduncle: the stalk holding up a whole flower cluster. Calyx: the small cup surrounding each flower base. Undulating: slightly wavy along the edge.

Source: P. H. Davis (ed.), Flora of Turkey and the East Aegean Islands, Volume 7, printed pages 76–78, genus 4, Lavandula. Original classification and measurements retained; explanations simplified for readers aged about 16.
