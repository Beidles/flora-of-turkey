How to use

This key is for plants already identified as Melissa. Flora of Turkey treats the Turkish plants as one species, Melissa officinalis, with three subspecies. Use a flowering plant and a hand lens. Examine several stem sections and leaves, not just one. At each step, tap the description that fits best. Follow the choices until you reach a subspecies (a named group within a species). If a feature is unclear, use Go back and compare both routes. Choose a route only when the features fit together. If the hairs, leaf base and flower cup disagree, the plant may be intermediate between subspecies; do not force a name.

Possible identification

Check all the features before deciding. Intermediate plants occur between all three subspecies in Flora of Turkey. If your plant mixes their features, keep the identification as Melissa officinalis until it can be checked.

Words explained

Calyx: the green cup around the base of the flower. It has an upper and lower lip. Look for the small middle tooth on the upper lip: it may be obvious, tiny or absent. A hand lens helps.

Gland-tipped hairs: tiny hairs with a small rounded gland at the tip. Spreading hairs: longer hairs sticking out from the stem. Wedge-shaped leaf base: the leaf narrows toward its stalk. Flat base: the leaf ends almost straight across at the stalk. Heart-shaped base: two rounded lobes sit either side of the stalk. Felt-like: thick, matted hairs.

Before using this key: Melissa has broad, toothed leaves and small, two-lipped flowers in groups where upper leaves join the stem. Davis describes flowers about 8–16 mm long, pale yellow becoming white, sometimes tinged pale mauve. These are checking features, not a key separating Melissa from every similar genus. Smell is not used to choose a subspecies here.
